# Boldinbeauty: project positioning memo

## Approved facts and positioning foundation

### Takeaway
Boldinbeauty is documented as a 3D AAA-style gacha with a post-apocalyptic cyberpunk setting, sentient human/robot/angel operators, and a single active character switched from a three-operator lineup. These are intended-design facts, not proof of implementation or release readiness; market positioning language below remains a proposal until owner-approved.

### Cited Findings
- The title is **Boldinbeauty**; the project describes itself as a 3D AAA-style gacha and cites Genshin Impact, Zenless Zone Zero and Wuthering Waves as references. Playable characters are called **operators**, the player is a **commander**, and Luna is the central narrative character. The inventory is intended to be simple. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §1, lines 12–14](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L12)
- Setting: Tokyo, Japan, with a cyberpunk-like 2009 “Neo Tokyo” aesthetic; a 2002 catastrophe preceded the playable story spanning 2009–2017. Non-biblical angels of pure light and mathematics caused the end-of-world event. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §8, lines 134–138](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L134)
- Humanity survives underground in “the arc.” Operators were created to fight angels and are autonomous, sentient beings composed of 50% human, 49% robot and 1% Angel. Summoning represents recruitment. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §8, lines 138–140](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L138)
- Combat supports a three-operator lineup but only one operator is active at a time; the player switches operators and there are no accompanying AI teammates. Operators share movement and actions including jumping, climbing, swimming, blocking and parrying. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §6, lines 120–124](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L120)
- The world structure is Chapter → Stage/Level → Section; levels contain 2–5 sections and roughly 10–30 enemies per section, with a mini-boss each level and bosses at chapter endings. These are design statements; source does not say they have been implemented. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §7, lines 126–132](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L126)
- Equipment is optional and generally separate from operator identity and kits; signature weapons are the operator-linked exception and may alter looks, animations and moves. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §4, lines 84–93](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L84)
- The project defines rarity labels and summon odds: Rare 78%, Epic 19.9%, Legendary 2%, Secret 0.1%. Non-Daily pulls generate persistent Mileage spent manually; listed thresholds are 250 for a featured 5-star and 2,500 for a 6-star redemption when eligible. Daily tickets expire and do not earn Mileage or 5-/6-star pity credit. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §2, lines 25–53](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L25)
- The story trio Luna, Plack and Kuu is mostly present in story regardless of the selected gameplay operator; biographies, individual goals and move sets remain deferred. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §5, lines 95–102](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L95)
- Canon guardrail: design facts do not establish implementation; OPEN, WORKING and DEFERRED labels must remain unresolved as marked, and proposals become canon only after owner acceptance. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §0, lines 4–10](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L4)

### Inferences
- **Potential positioning territory (proposal):** “Build a squad of sentient operators to survive Neo Tokyo and fight mathematical angels.” This condenses setting and squad combat, but is not approved tagline/copy.
- Operator switching, the unusual angel ontology, and the government’s use of sponsorships create possible campaign hooks. Treat them as creative angles for testing, not proven differentiators or claims of unique market status.
- Gacha is explicit project identity, so acquisition and monetization should be communicated accurately and transparently; the source does not establish a target age, ethical promise, or final commercial design.

### Gaps
- No approved positioning statement, tagline, brand voice, key art, trailer, proof of gameplay, target audience demographics/geographies, competitor analysis, or validated differentiator is recorded.
- Single-player/multiplayer scope and first playable milestone are OPEN; no release date, player count, platform-by-platform device coverage, or implementation status is supplied. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §1, lines 15–23](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L15)
- Operator biographies, goals and move sets remain deferred; detailed angel origins, behaviour and vulnerabilities are deferred. These gaps constrain character- and enemy-led campaign claims. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §§5, 8, lines 95–102, 134–142](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L95)
- Several marketing-sensitive gacha details remain OPEN, including eligible pools, featured exclusivity, Standard pity application, pity carry-over and other counter interactions. Do not simplify into unverified store/FAQ promises. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §2, lines 33–53](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L33)

## Audience clues, differentiators and launch risks

### Takeaway
The source supports genre and tone clues, not a defined audience. The safest potential distinction to explore is the combination of sentient hybrid operators, mathematical angels, underground Neo Tokyo, and tactical one-at-a-time switching; perceived novelty and audience fit require research and playtest evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Genre/market frame: 3D AAA-style gacha, with three named games as references rather than explicit competitors or positioning benchmarks. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §1, lines 12–15](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L12)
- Tone and narrative material include cyberpunk-like Neo Tokyo, an extinction-scale angel catastrophe, humanity sheltering underground, and a mostly evil dictatorship exploiting operators for money and societal support through sponsorships. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §8, lines 134–142](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L134)
- The story’s central trio works under government, doing jobs, fighting angels and dealing with other operators’ problems; the player is a commander, but membership among government rulers is not established. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §8, lines 141–142](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L141)
- Combat’s three-character roster has only one present/active operator at a time, with button switching and no AI companions. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §6, lines 120–124](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L120)
- Platform list is Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and Apple mobile; whether Apple mobile means iOS is WORKING and exact device coverage OPEN. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §1, lines 14–16](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L14)

### Inferences
- Audience hypotheses for research (not established facts): players interested in character-collecting action RPGs, dystopian science-fantasy, and character-switch combat may be relevant cohorts. Validate via interviews, store-page tests and playable builds before narrowing targeting.
- A useful message order to test is: (1) setting/threat hook, (2) operator identity and switching combat, (3) narrative conflict and sponsorship/dictatorship, (4) gacha/progression specifics only where finalized. Lead first with accessible world and play, not statistical economy detail, unless the channel explicitly targets gacha-system comparison.
- The dictatorship/sponsorship premise may create a compelling critique of exploitation, while the product’s own gacha format could invite scrutiny about the relationship between fiction and monetization. This is a communications risk hypothesis, not a source-confirmed audience objection; address only with accurate, approved economy disclosures.
- Platform breadth should not be presented as a tested simultaneous launch commitment until platform scope/build evidence is confirmed.

### Gaps
- Audience age, gender, region, language/localization, spending profile, accessibility needs and player motivations are unspecified.
- No competitor positioning, demand validation, audience research, wishlists, community size, campaign performance or platform preference evidence is supplied.
- The source contains no approved language about monetization fairness, odds disclosure, spending controls, age rating, privacy, online requirements, or commercial availability.
- No proof is supplied that combat, environments, operators, animation changes or listed platforms are implemented, tested, or representative of final product. Section 12.5.32 explicitly requires feature claims to be implemented or clearly prospective and gameplay/media provenance to be accurate. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §12.5.32, lines 597–602](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L597)

## Proposed messaging hierarchy and launch assumptions

### Takeaway
The following hierarchy is a **proposal for testing**, not approved canon or final campaign copy. Launch communications should separate verified build facts from intended design, preserve unresolved assumptions, and avoid invented dates, player counts, testimonials or implied gameplay footage.

### Cited Findings
- Marketing work should be drafted against approved features and positioning, retaining platform limits and audience context. Feature claims must be implemented or clearly prospective; gameplay/media provenance must be accurate; do not invent release date, player count or testimonial. Drafting alone does not authorize posting, ad spend or store publication. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §12.5.32, lines 597–602](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L597)
- Project knowledge rules distinguish intended design from implementation evidence and require proposals to remain non-canon until owner acceptance. — [BoldInBeauty_MAIN §0, lines 4–9](../../BoldInBeauty_MAIN#L4)

### Inferences
**Proposed hierarchy (validate with audience tests and approved build evidence):**
1. **Hook:** Neo Tokyo after catastrophe, where mathematical angels threaten humanity’s last refuge.
2. **Player fantasy:** command a lineup of sentient hybrid operators built to fight the angels.
3. **Gameplay proof:** show one-at-a-time operator switching and real, representative combat footage once a build verifies it.
4. **Story tension:** explore operators serving an exploitative dictatorship and sponsorship economy, with clear tone framing so fiction is not mistaken for a real-world product promise.
5. **System details:** explain optional equipment/signature weapons and gacha odds, pity and Mileage accurately in FAQ/store sections after OPEN rules are resolved.
6. **Availability:** name supported platforms, date, price and online requirements only when approved and evidenced.

### Gaps
- The heading **11.32** does not exist in the supplied file. The relevant marketing guidance is **§12.5.32** (lines 597–602), which was used here; §11 contains completed artifacts and workflow guidance, including the operator template, with no subsection 11.32.
- First playable milestone, implementation state, multiplayer status, device support, release window, price, online/service requirements and launch platforms remain unconfirmed in the source; these are launch assumptions to resolve before public store copy.
- Open gacha-pool/pity interactions and pending operator lore constrain detailed system copy and character-led promises; see cited gaps above.
- No approved brand promise, audience, CTA, community platform, campaign budget, or publishing authorization is recorded.

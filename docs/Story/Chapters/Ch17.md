## Summary
A government court has summoned an operator for 'organising without a sponsor'. Judge is assigned to the bench because her name still means something. She has been the voice of law since before the government existed and she knows it. The chapter is her day: a run of cases across a courthouse that has been rigged for the sponsors, each one a small test of her impartiality. She disdains operators and says so, and still rules for the accused because the law as she carries it says to. The bailiff automata are ordered to remove her when the verdict goes against the government. She puts down the first with a gavel of solid metal and wires. In chambers she finds that the charges were drafted by someone she cannot identify, in a house style she has seen before. In the last case she rules that commanders are subject to the same law as anyone else. Luna reads the ruling that night, twice. Judge does not know that, and would not care.

## Key reveal
Judge's authority predates the government, and she can try anyone, including commanders.

# Ch 17 - Court in Session - Script

**Arc 2 / 2010 / SIDE STORY: Judge.** A government courthouse rigged for the sponsors. One day of trials from the bench. Formal,
dry, severe; ends with a precedent.

---

## Cold open (cinematic, 30 s)
A courthouse at dawn that smells of floor wax. A woman six feet five in a power dress, short white hair, visible robotic limbs, a metal
gavel with wires running through it, reading statutes to an empty chamber, from memory, in an old-country cadence.
JUDGE: "Article one. No person shall be held without cause. Article two. No cause shall be held without person."
*(A pause.)*
JUDGE: Article three was never ratified. It was merely *popular.*

## Episode 1 - "The Bench"
**Scene:** Courtroom 1. **Present:** Judge (playable guest). The Clerk (BLUE).

CLERK: Your Honour, the first case is the matter of the arc versus an operator.
JUDGE: Name?
CLERK: Withheld.
JUDGE: That is not a name.
CLERK: It is on the charge sheet.
JUDGE: Then the charge sheet is not a document. It is a *rumour.* (she takes the sheet) Charge: "Organising without a sponsor."
There is no such offence.
CLERK: It is a new offence, Your Honour.
JUDGE: There is no such thing as a *new* offence. There is only an *old* offence that someone has found a new use for.

**Gameplay:** Non-combat (PURPLE): the first trial as a dialogue scene: Judge asks three questions (choice-free, answers scripted).
**Beat:** Judge rules the charge void; the court is rattled.

## Episode 2 - "The Vending Machine"
**Scene:** Courtroom 2. **Present:** Judge. A vending machine witness.

JUDGE: The witness will state its name.
VENDING MACHINE: (a bright jingle) Refreshments.
JUDGE: Its *name.*
VENDING MACHINE: ...Drinks and snacks.
JUDGE: (to the clerk, without expression) I have been in this profession fifty-one years. I have taken testimony from a cat, a
priest and a drowned man, and I have never taken testimony from a beverage.
CLERK: It was in the room at the time of the incident.
JUDGE: So was the *wall.* (to the machine) Did you see the accused organise?
VENDING MACHINE: ...Would you like a drink?
JUDGE: (a very long breath) The witness is struck.

**Gameplay:** Courtroom puzzle: examine five pieces of evidence for contradictions.
**Beat:** A dry comedy beat. Judge's impartiality is introduced as a terrifying thing.

## Episode 3 - "The Cell Block"
**Scene:** Holding cells. **Present:** Judge. Bailiff automata (RED).

*(The accused is being moved out of the cells by three bailiffs at dawn, in cuffs, without a hearing.)*
JUDGE: Where are you taking them?
BAILIFF: Another court, Your Honour.
JUDGE: Which?
BAILIFF: ...A faster court.
JUDGE: There is no faster court. There is only a court that has stopped asking. (she steps between them) Release the accused.
BAILIFF: We have an order.
JUDGE: You have a *paper.* An order is something a court *gives.* A paper is something a person *forgot to read.* (the gavel rises, wired,
heavy) Release them.

**Gameplay:** Combat: Judge's kit (a guest operator): short fights in the cell block; free the accused.
**Beat:** Judge defends the accused with her own hands.

## Episode 4 - "House Style"
**Scene:** Chambers. **Present:** Judge.

*(She is reading the charge sheet again, under a lamp.)*
JUDGE: (to herself) "Organising without a sponsor." (she turns the page; stops) This is not the clerk's hand. The drafting is not the
clerk's. (she runs a finger along the margin) I have seen this style before. The short sentences. The past tense. Always speaking of
a person as though they were already *finished.*
CLERK: Your Honour?
JUDGE: Who drafted the charge?
CLERK: The Narrator's office, Your Honour. It was in the folder.
*(A pause. Her gavel's wires hum faintly.)*
JUDGE: The *Narrator.*
CLERK: Yes, Your Honour.
JUDGE: Then the charge was not written to be *tried.* It was written to be *believed.*

**Gameplay:** Chambers exploration; a documents puzzle; a hidden stamp (YELLOW).
**Beat:** **Midpoint swerve.** The charges are authored by a Secret's office.

## Episode 5 - "The Statute"
**Scene:** Chambers, back room. **Present:** Judge.

*(A glass case with a single sheet of paper, brown at the edges, in her own younger hand.)*
JUDGE: (reading) "On the second of November, 2002, in the absence of any other authority, I, Manton, sole surviving member of the
bar, do declare the following to be law." (she stops) ...Article one.
*(She is quiet for a while. The wires in the gavel hum.)*
JUDGE: I wrote this by a candle. I was sixty-seven years old, and I thought it would last a month.
CLERK: It has lasted seven years, Your Honour.
JUDGE: (very dry) Then it is an unusually durable *month.*

**Gameplay:** Non-combat (PURPLE): memory scene; a flashback montage of 2002.
**Beat:** Judge's law predates the government.

## Episode 6 - "The Bailiffs Disagree"
**Scene:** The courthouse atrium. **Present:** Judge.

BAILIFF PRIME: (arriving with six automata) Your Honour, you are ordered off the bench.
JUDGE: By whom?
BAILIFF PRIME: By the Council.
JUDGE: And what is the Council's authority over this court?
BAILIFF PRIME: (carefully) They hold the building.
JUDGE: (almost kindly) The building is not the court. The building is only where the court *sits.* (she steps to the bench) Court
is in session.
BAILIFF PRIME: Your Honour --
JUDGE: *Court is in session.*

**Gameplay:** Defensive stand: hold the bench through three waves of bailiff automata.
**Beat:** Judge refuses to be removed.

## Episode 7 - "The Last Case"
**Scene:** Courtroom 1, evening. **Present:** Judge, the accused (BLUE).

*(The accused, finally unmanacled, stands. The courtroom is half-wrecked. A gavel-staffed bailiff waits at the door.)*
ACCUSED: Your Honour. May I ask you something?
JUDGE: You may.
ACCUSED: Why did you help me? You don't *like* operators.
JUDGE: (a pause) That is correct. I do not.
ACCUSED: Then --
JUDGE: Liking has no standing in this court. (she looks at the room) I would no sooner convict you for being disliked than I would
convict a *bridge.*
ACCUSED: (a small smile) That's the nicest thing anybody has said to me.
JUDGE: It was not *nice.* It was *correct.* (she raises the gavel) The Bailiff Prime has declined to leave the room.

**Gameplay:** Mini-boss: the Bailiff Prime's honour guard.
**Beat:** **Low point.** The court's last stand.

## Episode 8 - "The Bailiff Prime"
**Scene:** Courtroom 1. Boss fight. **Present:** Judge.

*(Boss: the Bailiff Prime, a giant automaton with a gavel hammer. Phase 1, hammer slams; Phase 2, the room becomes a docket: each
swing "sentences" a bench to destruction.)*
BAILIFF PRIME: (a booming bell) *Order! Order! Order!*
JUDGE: (calmly) There *is* order. (she brings the metal gavel down on his leg joint) That was it.
*(He falls with the sound of a vault door.)*
JUDGE: (to the ruined court) The accused is acquitted. The charge is struck. The *drafter* is on notice.

**Gameplay:** Boss: dodge hammer swings; hit the leg joints; Judge's gavel as a wired electric finish.
**Beat:** Bailiff Prime falls; the verdict is entered.

**Stinger:** That night Judge dictates one final ruling to the empty room: "Commanders are subject to the same law as any person in
the arc." Somewhere across the city, Luna, in a bunk hall, reads the printed ruling twice. Judge does not know that, and would not
care.

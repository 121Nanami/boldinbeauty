# Ch 19 - House Rules (script)

**Status:** PROPOSED full script, 2026-10-11. Side story: **Gambler** (5-star operator, MAIN section 5). Characterisation is not documented; voice and manner here are PROPOSED. Gambler's voice: warm, unhurried, speaks in odds, never loses her temper, keeps a private count.

Chapter promise: someone is buying every bet against Luna, and the money has a wing on it.

---

## Episode 1 - The Deck

*The Deck, a casino on the arc's upper terrace, under a glass ceiling that shows a painted sky. Brass, felt, glass, a hundred tables. Gambler, in a waistcoat and rolled sleeves, deals herself a hand she will not play.*

**[Cold open. Warm lounge jazz, slightly out of tune. The camera drifts through the room and lands on Gambler.]**

GAMBLER: *(to the empty chair across from her)* Two of clubs. Four of hearts. *(she turns a third card, smiles)* You never could bluff, could you?

*A barman in a bow tie sets a drink at her elbow.*

BARMAN: Boss. The board's moved.

GAMBLER: *(not looking up)* How far?

BARMAN: Luna's at forty to one.

GAMBLER: *(a very small pause)* This morning?

BARMAN: Seventy.

GAMBLER: *(slowly)* Someone's buying.

*She lays down her cards.*

GAMBLER: Show me.

**[Tutorial: Gambler's combat uses a "hand" system: the player draws cards that grant abilities; odds shift in real time.]**

## Episode 2 - Odds

*A wall of chalkboards over the bar: a list of names, odds written in neat yellow script. "LUNA TO SURVIVE THE YEAR: 40/1." "GOVERNMENT TO FALL: 200/1." "SECRETS TO UNIFY: 999/1."*

GAMBLER: *(standing in front of it, hands in pockets)* Who's been laying?

BARMAN: One account. Unnamed. Daily. Each bet's the same: against.

GAMBLER: How much?

BARMAN: *(quietly)* All of it.

*GAMBLER takes the chalk, steps up, and writes a new line: "WHO IS BETTING?" Then, below: "EVEN."*

BARMAN: Boss, that's not a market.

GAMBLER: *(mildly)* It's a question. Questions are always even.

**[Gameplay: investigation hub. The player walks the casino floor, talks to dealers, collects chips, and cross-references account numbers.]**

*A dealer at the roulette table, nervous, polishing a chip:*

DEALER: The money comes through a reserve account. No fund. No name.

GAMBLER: *(thoughtfully)* Where's the reserve?

DEALER: *(whispering)* Upstairs. The vault.

## Episode 3 - Chips

*The vault, below the gaming floor. A round steel door, a keypad, a ring of guards. GAMBLER, with a deck in one hand, a smile on her face, saunters up to the lead guard.*

GAMBLER: Evening, Tomo. How's your mother?

TOMO: *(softening)* Better, boss.

GAMBLER: *(warmly)* I'm glad.

*She slips a single chip into his palm. He looks down. It is a gold chip, stamped with a wing.*

TOMO: *(low)* Where'd you get this?

GAMBLER: *(lightly)* I didn't. It was in the till. Came in with this week's float.

TOMO: *(very quietly)* These don't circulate.

GAMBLER: No. They don't.

*TOMO looks at the chip. Then at the vault door. Then at GAMBLER.*

TOMO: *(unlocking it)* You didn't see me.

GAMBLER: *(patting his arm)* I never do.

**[Puzzle: chip-count. A vault ledger has twelve columns; the player must match chips to accounts by weight, colour and stamp. A single wing-stamped chip appears in the wrong column.]**

## Episode 4 - The Wing (Midpoint)

*Inside the vault. Rows of safe-deposit boxes in brass and steel. At the centre, a single table with a ledger and a stack of chips, every one stamped with the same three-feathered wing.*

GAMBLER: *(running a finger along the ledger)* Seventy-three thousand, four hundred. Every week. For eleven months. *(she looks up)* It's not a fund. It's an allowance.

*She turns the page. The last column, labelled "PURPOSE", reads, in small neat letters: "RISK HEDGE: SUBJECT L."*

GAMBLER: *(very softly)* Subject L.

*A silence. The barman, at the door, shifts uneasily.*

BARMAN: Boss?

GAMBLER: *(not turning)* They're not betting against her. *(a pause)* They're insuring.

BARMAN: Against what?

GAMBLER: *(closing the ledger)* Against her *winning*.

*She puts the ledger back where she found it, very precisely, and straightens the chips.*

GAMBLER: *(a low laugh)* Well. That *is* interesting.

## Episode 5 - The Visitor

*The gaming floor, late. A visitor in a long coat, hood up, takes a seat at a quiet table in a corner. A waiter approaches.*

WAITER: Can I get you anything?

LUNA: *(from under the hood)* A coffee. And a hand.

*GAMBLER, at the bar, glances over, and stops. She finishes her drink, sets it down, and crosses the floor.*

GAMBLER: *(sitting across from her)* Evening, Luna.

LUNA: *(lowering her hood)* You know me.

GAMBLER: *(smiling)* The whole house knows you. *(she deals two cards)* Forty to one.

LUNA: *(looking at her cards)* I'd have thought it was higher.

GAMBLER: It was seventy this morning.

LUNA: *(a half-laugh)* Flattering.

GAMBLER: *(not smiling)* It isn't. Somebody's laying a fortune against you, and I don't think they want you to lose.

*LUNA's hand pauses.*

LUNA: What do they want?

GAMBLER: *(quietly)* To make sure that when you win, they've collected.

**[Gameplay: a card-game scene. The player plays a short hand of poker against Gambler, with dialogue triggered by each round. Win or lose, the dialogue continues.]**

## Episode 6 - The Card

*Gambler deals the last card. LUNA turns hers. GAMBLER, without a word, turns hers. They are both the same, the ace of spades.*

GAMBLER: *(softly)* Push.

LUNA: *(smiling faintly)* I like it.

*She reaches into her coat, and takes out a small object, a playing card, face down, a little creased from her pocket. She sets it on the table, between them.*

LUNA: Do you know this?

*GAMBLER looks at it a moment, then, with one finger, turns it over. A blank white face, a stylised J in the corner.*

*She does not touch it again.*

GAMBLER: *(a long pause)* That's not mine.

LUNA: I didn't think it was.

GAMBLER: *(slowly)* It's not any house's.

LUNA: Whose is it?

*GAMBLER leans back in her chair, folding her hands.*

GAMBLER: There's only one person in the arc who leaves cards behind. *(a pause)* They never sit at a table. They don't gamble. *(she smiles, a little sadly)* They say it's because the house always loses to them.

LUNA: *(very quietly)* The Jester.

GAMBLER: *(nodding)* If you hold that, you're holding an invitation.

## Episode 7 - Dealer's Choice

*The rooftop of the Deck. The painted sky has been rolled back, and a real, grey one shows through. A helicopter pad, cranes, a ring of enforcers. A tall man in a tuxedo, a deck of cards fanned in each hand, steps forward. The BACKER'S DEALER.*

**[Low point. The Dealer upends a table, and the casino catches fire.]**

DEALER: *(in a smooth, casual voice)* The house has been very patient. *(he flicks a card; it embeds in the wall at LUNA's ear)* But the house's patience is not a toy.

GAMBLER: *(stepping forward)* Julian.

DEALER: *(glancing at her)* Ma'am.

GAMBLER: You work for me.

DEALER: *(politely)* Not tonight.

*A silence. A single card whistles past, shearing off a lock of GAMBLER's hair.*

GAMBLER: *(very softly)* Well. I suppose that settles the odds.

*She turns her chair over with a kick, and a hail of cards bursts from her sleeves.*

## Episode 8 - The Backer's Dealer (Boss)

**[Boss: The Backer's Dealer. Phase 1: razor cards in fans; Gambler's hand system can counter with matching suits. Phase 2: the rooftop's cranes swing chandeliers; use card-throws to cut the chains. Phase 3: he reveals a rotating blade-deck mounted to his arm; break it by playing a "straight".]**

*The fight is flamboyant. GAMBLER and the DEALER trade cards across the burning roof, each volley a tiny duel. LUNA, at the edge, is a force of nature, a black hole bending the DEALER's thrown cards back at him.*

*At the end, he stands in the ruin of the helicopter pad, deck in tatters, a single card between two fingers.*

DEALER: *(grinning, bleeding)* You've no idea who you've angered.

GAMBLER: *(quietly)* I have a fair idea.

*She takes the card from his fingers, turns it, and drops it at his feet. It is blank.*

GAMBLER: *(gently)* Fold, Julian.

*He folds.*

### Stinger

*The wreck of the casino, dawn. LUNA and GAMBLER stand on the roof, looking over a smoking city. Behind them, the board, charred, the odds still legible: "LUNA TO SURVIVE THE YEAR: 12/1."*

GAMBLER: *(lighting a cigarette from a still-burning chandelier)* You've been climbing.

LUNA: *(dry)* It's a long way down.

GAMBLER: *(a smile)* What are the odds on you, now?

LUNA: I don't know. What are they?

*GAMBLER takes a long drag. Exhales.*

GAMBLER: I'm not going to tell you.

LUNA: *(a beat)* Why not?

GAMBLER: *(looking at the board)* Because a player who knows the odds stops playing the game.
